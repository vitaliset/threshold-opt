Classification in two stages

Let $(X, Y) \sim \mathbb{P}$, with features $X \in \mathcal{X}$ and label $Y \in \mathcal{Y} = \{0, \dots, K-1\}$. A classifier $h \colon \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ is a composition $h(x) = \delta(\hat{\mathbf{p}}(x))$ of two stages.

  • Inference. An estimator $\hat{\mathbf{p}} \colon \mathcal{X} \to \Delta^{K-1}$ of the conditional class probabilities $\eta_k(x) = \mathbb{P}(Y = k \mid X = x)$, where $\Delta^{K-1} = \{\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^K \mid p_k \geq 0, \, \sum_k p_k = 1\}$ is the simplex.
  • Decision. A decision rule $\delta \colon \Delta^{K-1} \to \mathcal{Y}$. The default is argmax, $\delta(\mathbf{p}) = \arg\max_k p_k$. The costs of errors enter only at this stage.
  • Two stages: estimate the probabilities, then decide.
  • The second stage is usually left at its default, argmax.
  • The dissertation is about the second stage.

Learning the partition

A partition matrix $\mathbf{A}$ takes the place of $\mathbf{C}$ in the Bayes rule (O'Brien et al., 2008): \[ \delta_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{p}) = \arg\min_{i} \sum_j A_{ji} \, p_j . \] It is chosen like the threshold, among $T$ random candidates. Three classes:

  • Separate the matrix that decides, $\mathbf{A}$, from the one that evaluates, $\mathbf{C}$, and fit $\mathbf{A}$ on held-out data.
  • Same recipe as the threshold: score candidates on the validation sample, keep the best; here $T$ random draws.
  • Shift: $K - 1$ offsets, no turning. Scaling: $K - 1$ weights, the boundaries turn. Full matrix: $K^2$ entries, any convex polyhedral partition.

The sequential cascade

A rule used with class-imbalanced data tests the classes in a priority order, rarest first, and predicts the first whose probability reaches its threshold, or the argmax if none does. It is a decision list (Rivest, 1987): \[ \delta_{\mathrm{ord}, \boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mathbf{p}) = \mathrm{ord}(j^*) , \quad j^* = \min \{ j \mid p_{\mathrm{ord}(j)} \geq \tau_{\mathrm{ord}(j)} \} . \]

Its regions are not convex and depend on the order of the tests.

  • One-vs-rest tests in a priority order, rarest class first; argmax only as the default.
  • A class tested early can claim points where another class has the larger probability.
  • Non-convex, order-dependent regions; in the benchmark it is SequentialSelection.

Two multiclass threshold rules

For $K = 3$ under $0/1$ loss, with the argmax boundaries dashed. Multidimensional thresholding keeps every boundary parallel to its argmax position; under probability-ratio thresholding the boundary between two classes is a line through the vertex of the third.

  • Two rules from the literature, one threshold per class; the next table places them.
  • Thresholds in differences: the boundaries translate. Thresholds in ratios: they turn around a vertex.
  • Neither turns each boundary on its own.

Proposition (6.2.3 and 6.2.4; proof: P5)

Under $0/1$ loss, probability-ratio thresholding with thresholds $\tau_k > 0$ is equivalent to linear scaling with $\beta_k = 1/\tau_k$, and multidimensional thresholding with $\boldsymbol{\tau} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ to the parallel shift with $\alpha_k = \tau_k$.

  • Five rules in use; four fall into the two matrix classes, the cascade into none, so no capacity analysis covers it.
  • Shifts translate the cost boundaries; weights also turn them, through $K - 1$ coupled parameters.
  • None turns each boundary on its own: that is what the prototypes add.
